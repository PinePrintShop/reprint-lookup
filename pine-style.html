Colors

From the Pine Brand Standards: black, white, cream and Pantone 107 yellow. Meaning comes from fill and shape (yellow = needs you, black = done), not from more colors. Red appears only when something is wrong: an error, lost money, data that won't save.

Type

Poppins Black for screen titles and the one big number on a screen. Helvetica for everything else. No typewriter font; numbers line up with even-width digits instead. Nothing smaller than 12px, so it reads on an iPad at arm's length.

Rules
  1. One yellow main button per screen.
  2. Only four colors. Yellow means "needs you" or "do this". Black means done. Red only for a real problem.
  3. Every app opens with the same black top bar: Pine logo, app name, version, practice pill, then that app's controls.
  4. Poppins Black only for titles and one big number, always in capitals and never squeezed. Everything else Helvetica. No Courier.
  5. Nothing smaller than 12px. Touch targets at least 44px; 56px on the floor iPads.
  6. Order and imprint IDs are always the black-and-yellow badge.
  7. Pop-ups and toasts look and behave the same in every app.
  8. The bar always runs: logo, app name, version, practice, the app's own views, then Refresh and ⚙ on the right. Token and practice live inside ⚙.
  9. One filter row under the bar: search, chips on one line (they scroll sideways), sort on the right.
  10. Lists use one card: ID, name and one sub-line, numbers on the right, one action. Tags on a second row. It opens in place, one at a time.
  11. Yellow is your next move: one yellow button per screen, and yellow tags only for "needs you". Card actions are black or outlined; facts are cream.
Moving the apps over

Each app switches to this style in its next version, like the self-update rule. No big rewrite.
